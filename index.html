    Frequently Asked Question
    My connection is so slow!
If all your browsing is slow, then signal strength could be an issue. However, if one site is slow, but others react normally, it is not your connection. The site you’re downloading from or visiting may just be experiencing a large amount of traffic and is not coping.
    Why do I keep getting disconnected?
This is normally due to line interference or an Internet Explorer setting. If your line is not faulty; the problem lies with Internet Explorer.
One of the settings in Internet Explorer controls the period of time that you would like your computer to continue maintaining a connection to the Internet. If your connection is idle, that is, there is no Internet going to or from your Service Provider, Internet Explorer automatically disconnects you from the Internet, which is inherited from the days of dial-up connections and prevented you from running up a massive phone bill.
    When I click a link, I get the error message ‘404 page not found’.
This does not mean there is anything wrong with your computer! The web page that you are trying to visit does not exist. It may have existed under the link that you clicked, but it is no longer there. This is called a broken link.

What do I do if I cannot access the internet?

  1. Check the network signal strength.
  2. Check whether you have activated the wireless online services.
  3. If the wireless online service is activated, check the network settings.
  4. If you are still unable to connect, contact us.

    Glossary
MTN broadband and cellphone technologies:

GPRS: This is the oldest and slowest technology of them all, but it is still faster than a dial-up connection, and this is where the mobile internet started. It has the widest coverage in the country and allows you to always be connected. GPRS+ is an enhancement of GPRS and is faster. If the other protocols are not supported in the area you are in, your connection will automatically select either of these protocols.

EDGE: This is up to four times faster than a normal dial-up connection. It is convenient and reliable.

3G : This is a very commonly supported protocol which stands for 3rd Generation and is up to six times faster than dial-up and allows you to even make video calls.

HSUPA/HSDPA: On most protocols, upload speed (speed of sending information to the internet) and download speed (speed of receiving information from the internet) often differ, uploading usually being slower than downloading. HSUPA enhances the speed of uploading Internet, for example, when sending large emails and attachments. HSDPA in turn enhances the speed of downloading files or pages from the internet.

APN: This allows a connection to the network, and it identifies the Internet entrance and exit associated with your account.

Authentication: This session tells the device which access number to use. You can store multiple access point names here.

Authentication Protocol Settings: Here you can control the authentication parameters.

Network type: This is your preferred connection type.

Band: This is your network technology. MTN automatically finds the strongest available band; however, you can set a band as your preference, for example, 3G, HSDPA, GPRS or EDGE.

Profile name: This is your preferred connection setting.

IP address: This is the number assigned to each device on the MTN network, which is active on a supporting network.

WINS settings: These are your internal connection settings.

DNS settings: These are your outgoing server settings.

Fair use Policy: We want you to enjoy your time/experience on the internet, and our fair use policy is there to make sure you have the best experience. There might be some customers who use so much data that it affects the quality of everyone else's surfing. We also want to make sure everyone gets the same value from their chosen price plan. This is why our fair use policy sets a very generous usage guideline. We understand that everyone goes over their limits sometimes, so if you exceed, fair use policy ensures no extra charges to sneak onto your bill.

WAN: Is an acronym for "Wide Area Network." It's computer networks that extend a large area, and connects several local-area networks (LANs). Here you can set up your WAN network.

LAN: A local area network is a group of computers and associated devices that share a common communications line.

DHCP: (Dynamic Host Configuration Protocol) is a communications protocol that lets network administrators centrally manage and automate the assignment of Internet Protocol (IP) addresses.

WLAN: Wireless local-area network, this is a type of local-area network that uses high-frequency radio waves rather than wires to communicate between nodes.
